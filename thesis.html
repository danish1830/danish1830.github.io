01

Aim & Hypothesis

Research Aims

  • Detect & explain regime shifts in glacier–climate relationships (pre-2000 vs post-2000).
  • Develop an empirical-statistical model framework for glacier mass balance.

Study Period

  • Mass-balance observations spanning 1950–2023 from 11 (eleven) Norwegian glaciers.
  • ERA5 reanalysis, CMIP6 projections & NVE observational data.

Hypothesis

"The turn of the century marks the transition to a new glacier regime, characterised by a change in the climatic controls on annual mass balances."

Figure 01 — Mass Balance Time Series

Cumulative Bₐ (m w.e.) for 11 Norwegian glaciers from 1950 to 2020. The dashed vertical line at 2000 indicates a marked acceleration in cumulative mass loss for most glaciers after 2000, signalling a transition toward increasingly negative mass balance regimes in the 21st century.

02

Methods

Part A

Regime Detection

Data sources: ERA5 reanalysis (ECMWF), CMIP6 projections, and glacier mass-balance observations (NVE).

  • Exploratory data analysis of glacier mass-balance time series (1950–2023).
  • Empirical Orthogonal Function (EOF) analysis to identify dominant modes of variability (NAO, EA, SCAN).
  • Detection of regime shifts using correlation testing and comparing pre-2000 & post-2000 periods.

Part B

Statistical Modelling

Empirical-statistical downscaling framework for mass balance prediction.

  • Predictor selection followed by training and testing with cross-validation.
  • Empirical-Statistical Downscaling via a pyESD based ML framework with 6 regressors (ARD, RidgeCV, RandomForest, Bagging, Stacking) & 3 selectors (Recursive, Tree-Based, Sequential).
  • Model evaluation using cross-validation and performance metrics (RMSE, R²).

Figure 02 — Modelling Routine Schematic

Predictors constructed from ERA5 datasets generate empirical-statistical models and their metrics. The best-performing models, individually or as an ensemble, can be coupled with a GCM to produce future projections. Data sources: Mass balance [NVE] & ERA5 Reanalysis [ECMWF].

03

Results & Findings

Part A · Regime Detection

Key Findings

  • Major regime shift identified around 2000 for most glaciers.
  • Post-2000: shift from winter (Bw) → summer (Bs) dominated control.
  • Shift from winter precipitation (NAO) → temperature-dominant controls.
  • Maritime glaciers: more precipitation sensitive. Continental glaciers: more temperature sensitive.
Weaker NAO (↓ Pacc) + higher Tabl → Most negative balance on record post-2000.

Part B · Statistical Modelling

Model Performance

  • Linear models (ARD, RidgeCV, BYR) performed ≥ non-linear models (Bag, RF, XGBoost).
  • More complete records pre-2000 → better predictive skill.
  • Maritime glaciers: higher performance. Continental glaciers: lower performance.
Incorporating proxy data + robust dataset → improved model performance.

Figure 03 — Regime Shift Scatter Comparison

(a) Scatterplots of Bₐ correlated with Bw (pre-2000) and Bs (post-2000). (b) Correlation of Bₐ with Bs post-2000. A general shift from winter-dominated to summer-dominated mass balance control is observed in 9 of 11 glaciers post-2000.
