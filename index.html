About

I am a recent graduate of the Erasmus Mundus Master's in Marine Environment — a joint international programme spanning Université de Bordeaux (France), Universidad del País Vasco (Spain), and Université de Liège (Belgium).

My academic background bridges marine and climate sciences, combining expertise in oceanography, data science, statistics and modelling. I completed my Master's thesis at the University of Glasgow — School of Geographical and Earth Sciences under the supervision of Dr. rer. nat. habil. Sebastian G. Mutz.

I am now an incoming PhD student at the Institute of Oceanography, National Taiwan University, where I will reconstruct Holocene hydroclimate variability around Taiwan through multi-proxy biomarker analysis of marine sediment cores, combined with downscaled climate-model comparisons.

Beyond academia, I find myself equally captivated by photography, video games, football (a devoted Real Madrid supporter), cooking, and reading. Currently exploring philosophy — specifically working through The Myth of Sisyphus by Albert Camus.

"Detection and Explanation of Regime Shifts in the Climatic Controls of Norwegian Glaciers"

Master's research at the University of Glasgow's Climate Dynamics Lab. Investigated how the dominant atmospheric and oceanic drivers governing Norwegian glacier mass balance have shifted across the 20th and 21st centuries.
